AITeaching privacy policy

Last updated: 8 November 2026

AITeaching is a work-recording tool that Fidorn, Inc. ("we") uses internally. It has two parts: the AITeaching desktop app for Windows and Mac, and the AITeaching browser extension for Chrome and Edge. This page explains what they record, where it goes, how long it is kept, who can see it, and how to stop it. It follows version 0.12 of the AITeaching recording agreement, which is the full and binding list.

Who uses AITeaching

Only our own staff and invited testers who have accepted the AITeaching recording agreement. A person accepts it by downloading and installing the desktop app; the app shows no agreement window and asks nothing when it starts. A new version of the agreement is accepted the same way, by installing the version that carries it. The extension is listed privately (Unlisted on the Chrome Web Store, Hidden on Edge Add-ons) and is useless without the desktop app.

Nothing is recorded on a computer where AITeaching is not installed. Once installed, AITeaching records around the clock: it starts by itself every time the person signs in and keeps running in the background until it is paused, quit or removed. When an update brings a new version of the agreement, the app shows nothing on the computer either: each version is published on our website, where it is signed.

What the browser extension records

On the web pages a person works in, in Chrome or Edge, the extension notes:

  • the full address of each page, including everything after the "?". Such addresses can carry sign-in links, password-reset links, one-time links from e-mails and the identifiers of the records opened; they are kept exactly as they appear, with nothing removed or hidden;
  • the page title;
  • the fields, buttons and labels on the page, which one the person used, and the value typed into an ordinary field;
  • the page's readable text, its contents and structure, and how they changed while the person worked;
  • which browser tab and window was in use.

The recording agreement also lets the extension note every tab open in that browser and the names of downloaded files, once a version that records them is installed and we turn it on for a computer (see "What the desktop app records" below).

The value of a password field, a card number, a card security code, a one-time code, or a hidden or file field is never recorded: the extension masks it in the browser's memory before anything is passed on. It does not filter out personal websites. The extension sends nothing over the internet and saves no credential to browser storage. It keeps a random id for the browser session, which the browser clears when it closes, plus a non-secret preference for the saved-login destination. It hands what it notes to the AITeaching desktop app on the same computer.

Saved logins

AITeaching includes a visible, user-controlled password manager. After a login succeeds, it is saved automatically, and a visible notice appears on the page naming the website and whether the login was saved or its password updated. The notice has a one-click Undo that removes the new login or restores the previous password, and it stays until the person dismisses it. A person can list, edit and delete every saved login from the extension's options page at any time. When the notice cannot be shown on a page, the desktop app asks “Save this login?” first instead. Filling is never silent: a page offers a “Fill saved login” button, and the person must click it. Desktop-app filling likewise shows a confirmation and targets only the named foreground application and its exact username and password controls.

Saved logins contain only a username or e-mail address and password. AITeaching rejects one-time-code and MFA seeds, does not save banking logins, and never reconstructs a password from keys or recordings. A saved login travels through a separate native helper and never enters recording events, page snapshots, screenshots, video, audio or logs.

The default destination is AWS Secrets Manager in our AWS account in the United States, encrypted under a separate company-specific AWS KMS key and namespaced to the signed-in Sam person and this computer. The opt-in local destination is Windows Credential Manager under the person's Windows account. Neither destination shares a login with another person, computer or company, and Fidorn staff cannot view it through the recording tools.

What the desktop app records

The desktop app records what the recording agreement lists:

  • screenshots of the display that holds the window in use, taken when something happens (switching app or window, a click, scrolling stops, typing pauses) and about every 5 seconds otherwise. We can instead set a computer to take them at a steady rate of up to 5 per second; we set that rate remotely, without asking again, and never above 5 per second. Identical pictures are skipped;
  • pictures of every other screen connected to the computer, at the same moments and with the same settings, just as sharp, so their text can be read. Nothing on them is hidden or blurred, and a picture of a screen that has not changed is skipped. We can turn this off for a computer;
  • where windows and screens sit: the position and size of the window in front, so a screenshot can be cropped to it; and the position, size, scaling and rotation of every screen, which one is the main screen, and which screen each click and scroll was on;
  • every keystroke, with the modifier keys held and the resulting text. A key typed into a password field is masked on the computer before it is saved: only the fact that a key was pressed is kept;
  • mouse clicks (position and button) and scrolling;
  • the names of the apps used and the titles of their windows;
  • the structure and visible text of the focused window (its buttons, fields, menus and labels), leaving out password fields;
  • what the browser extension hands it (above);
  • the computer's time zone, each time recording starts: its difference from UTC and, where the computer gives one, the zone's name;
  • the person's name, if one was typed in an earlier Setup of the computer. The team can change it;
  • a label with the lesson's name, when Teach is started for the person in Sam, our portal. The computer shows a notice when this happens. Teach only adds a label: AITeaching records all the time either way.

The agreement also covers the items below. Each switches on only when a version of the app that records it is installed, and each but the controls of desktop programs only when we turn it on for a computer:

  • a continuous video of each screen, saved in half-hour files. Nothing in it is blurred, though password boxes show as dots, as they do on screen. When pictures of the other screens are turned off, it shows only the screen in use;
  • the buttons, boxes and menu items used in desktop programs outside the browser, with the values typed into them (never the value of a password box);
  • every open window: the program, title, position, size and state (for example minimised, or in front) of every window, not only the one in use, each time one opens, moves, changes or closes. In File Explorer, also the folder each window shows and the names of the files selected in it;
  • what the other windows show: the structure and visible text of the windows not in use, read when the person leaves a window and again when it changes, leaving out password fields;
  • what is in Excel workbooks: each open workbook's name and the folder it is in, its sheets, and the values and formulas in its cells, including the cells nobody changes, read when it is opened and again every few minutes, with each change made and, when it is known, the value it replaced;
  • copies of Excel workbooks: a complete copy of each workbook opened, taken when it is opened, after it is saved and when it is closed, up to the size limits we set for one file and for one day;
  • every browser tab, in a browser that has the extension: the full address and title of every open tab, not only the one in view, each time a tab opens, closes, moves or changes. The addresses are kept in full, with the same risks as the page addresses above. Also the names of downloaded files: the file name only, never the folder it is saved in;
  • what is copied: everything copied to the clipboard, in any program: text in full, larger copies such as pictures as files, and the names and places of copied files. This includes passwords and other secrets that are copied, for example from a password manager: nothing copied is left out or hidden. AITeaching's own saved logins never go through the clipboard: it fills them straight into the login fields, and its options page never shows a saved password.

Sound is recorded only when we turn it on for a computer, and never before the person has given written consent to it outside AITeaching. There are two kinds, each turned on separately: the sound the computer plays, from any program, calls included, so the voices of the other people on a call are recorded, with which programs played sound each second and how loud (personal calls, music and videos are not left out); and everything the computer's microphone picks up while AITeaching is recording, which includes anyone speaking near the computer, in the room or on a call through its speakers. We tell the people on calls that calls may be recorded, by a standard line in our meeting invitations and e-mail signatures. An administrator can turn either off for a computer in Sam, and off in Sam is always off. While the microphone is on, the AITeaching icon says so, and "Pause sound" stops sound for a while. Nobody listens live: sound is never part of live watching. Sound is never scanned by the AI model or copied into Box; only the team that builds playbooks can listen to it, and it is kept like the rest of the recording.

Personal apps and websites are not filtered out: everything on every recorded screen is captured, whatever the app, including personal e-mail, banking and shopping, with their pages, their addresses and their contents. Do not do private things on a computer while it is recording, or remove AITeaching first. The recording agreement is the full and binding list.

Each computer also sends us a regular status report so the team can see that it is working: for example, whether AITeaching is paused, the person's name if one was typed, and the name, size and fingerprint of any file it could not upload because one of the same name is already stored, so that a file already stored as an exact copy can be cleared from the computer.

Live watching

The recording agreement also lets the team watch a computer's screens live while AITeaching runs, in our team-only viewer: the screen in use and, smaller, every other screen connected to it. Watching saves nothing beyond the normal recording, and the AITeaching icon shows when someone is watching. The live picture travels encrypted, either straight to the viewer or through relay servers of Amazon Web Services.

Where the recording goes

  1. On the computer. Recordings are first saved in a local folder on the recorded computer (the recording buffer).
  2. To our cloud storage. As each file is finished, the desktop app uploads it to a storage bucket we control in Amazon Web Services (AWS), in the United States, where the internal team that builds playbooks can review it. It travels encrypted and is stored encrypted. The local copy is removed only after the upload is confirmed. Status reports go to the same place. A daily backup copy is kept in a separate, encrypted backup store in the same AWS account.
  3. Processing. A processing service that we run in our own AWS account copies each recording's events and noted items, as they are, into tables in its own encrypted storage there (not its pictures, page recordings, video or sound), which only our administrators can query. An earlier processing service, turned off today, copied the recordings to its own disk and turned them into step-by-step drafts and playbooks.
  4. AI. For recordings made under version 0.5 of the recording agreement or later, the recordings, screenshots and page contents included, are read by an AI model, Claude Sonnet, made by Anthropic, through Amazon Bedrock under our own AWS account, processed in AWS data centres in the United States, to find and describe the steps of each task. What it writes follows the same rules as everything else we keep. Pictures of the other screens, the screen video and sound are never read by the AI model and never copied into our Box folder; only the internal team that builds playbooks sees or hears them.
  5. Our viewer. The team looks at recordings, and at each computer's status and name, in a web viewer that we run in our own AWS account and that only our team can open.
  6. Drafts and playbooks. The resulting drafts and playbooks are stored in our company account on Box, together with a small file for each computer that holds its ID and the person's name, if one was typed (never in a folder's name).

We do not sell recordings or anything made from them, and we do not share them with anyone outside our company except the service providers named above (AWS, including Amazon Bedrock, and Box), which hold or process them for us. Recordings are used only to build and review work playbooks.

How long it is kept

  • On the computer: until the file is uploaded and the upload is confirmed. Anything not yet uploaded when AITeaching is removed stays on the computer until someone deletes it.
  • Tables on the processing service: until deleted. Today no automatic deletion is set for them; the results of queries on them are deleted after 7 days. The earlier processing service is turned off; its working copies were deleted automatically, within about a week.
  • Recording files in cloud storage, the screen video and sound included, and status reports: until deleted. Today no automatic deletion is set for any of them. The recording agreement also allows us to keep recordings in cloud storage for only about 7 days, while a separate, encrypted archive keeps them until we delete them or the engagement ends (an older day is brought back from the archive when it is needed); removing them from cloud storage after about 7 days is planned and not switched on yet.
  • Backup copies: each daily backup is kept 35 days, so a deleted file can remain in a backup for up to 35 days.
  • Drafts, playbooks and the per-computer files in Box: until deleted. Nothing deletes them automatically, and the team deletes them when they are no longer needed.
  • Saved logins: until the person deletes them, rotates them at the source, or removes AITeaching. Uninstall asks whether to retain them and defaults to deleting both cloud and local entries; choosing Yes explicitly retains them for a reinstall.

Who can see it

Only the internal team at Fidorn, Inc. that builds and reviews playbooks, and the systems described above, can see recordings. Saved logins are different: only the linked person's workflow identity on that same computer can retrieve them; they are not exposed to the recording viewer, recordings pipeline, colleagues, other firms or Fidorn staff.

How to stop

  • For a while, on Windows: right-click the AITeaching icon near the clock. "Pause" stops recording until you choose "Resume", or for 60 minutes; anyone at the computer may pause, and while it is paused the team sees "Paused since …" next to the computer. "Quit AITeaching" stops recording at once, until the next time you sign in: AITeaching sends what it can of what it has already recorded, then closes, and anything not yet sent stays on the computer until AITeaching next runs.
  • Only sound, for a while, on Windows: right-click the AITeaching icon and choose "Pause sound". No sound is recorded and the microphone is not used until you choose "Resume sound", or for 60 minutes; the rest of the recording goes on.
  • For good: remove AITeaching. Recording stops and AITeaching no longer starts when you sign in. On Windows, uninstall also asks whether to keep saved logins and defaults to deleting them; an offline deletion failure stops uninstall so it does not silently leave cloud credentials behind.
    • Windows: Settings, Apps, AITeaching, Uninstall.
    • Mac: in Terminal, run the removal line from the AITeaching Mac install guide, bash ~/Downloads/install-aiteaching.sh --uninstall.
    Remove the extension in the browser as well (Chrome: chrome://extensions; Edge: edge://extensions).
  • Removing AITeaching does not delete what was already recorded, whether it was uploaded or is still waiting on the computer. To ask for your recordings to be deleted, or for any question about this policy, contact bao@fidorn.com.

Changes to this policy

If we change what is recorded or where it goes, we update this page and the recording agreement. The new terms apply once you install the version of the desktop app that carries them: installing it accepts them.
